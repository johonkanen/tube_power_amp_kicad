# Rev C bias channel: ngspice results

One bias channel of sheet 02 (channel 1 as generated by `rev_c/tools/sheets/draw_all.py` `sheet02`), with
its -80 V input, VREF2V5 and the tube grid network of sheet 01. ngspice-47. Simulated 2026-11-25.

Rerun with `python3 rev_c/sim/opamp/run_sims.py [dc loop step ac pulse powerup loss diss derate]`
(about 10 minutes for all). The runner writes the netlists to `netlists/`, the numbers to `summary.json`,
and the three plots next to this file.

## Model

**TI OPA462 PSpice model**, `rev_c/sim/models/opa462_ti/` (TI lit SBOMAV9, "Final 1.3", 02SEP2022,
Green-Williams-Lis architecture, five pins). It runs unmodified in ngspice-47 with `ngbehavior=ps`. TI lists
what it models: Aol and phase with RL / CL, GBW, CMRR / PSRR vs frequency, Zo vs frequency, output swing vs
current, Isc, Iq, slew rate, overload recovery, Vos and drift, input CM range, and the ESD cells.

`opa462_ed.lib` wraps it with two additions:

- **E/D pin**, which TI's model doesn't have. The output is disabled for V(E/D) - V(E/D Com) below
  0.35 V and enabled above 0.8 V (datasheet 6.5). Disabled, the output is the 160 kΩ / 36 pF the datasheet
  gives, and the amplifier behind it keeps running (datasheet 7.3.4). With E/D tied to +5 V, the part enables
  when +5 V passes about 0.6 V.
- **Iq at low total supply.** TI's model draws a constant 3.2 mA from V+ to V- even at 0 V supply. The wrapper
  fades it to zero below 5 V total.

Other modelling: ADR4525 as a 2.5 V source that follows +5 V minus 0.5 V of dropout (zero below 1.2 V), with
1 Ω and C204. The -80 V input is J201 → 10R → M80 with the S1G clamp, 100 µF (0.1 Ω ESR) and 100 nF. The
other three channels plus dividers are a load that brings the rail to 15 mA. The grid network is the PI
plate (280 V DC, 40 kΩ), the 220 nF, the 91k grid leak, the 5.6k stopper, 15 pF, and the grid-cathode diode
(1 nA, n = 2, 500 Ω).

**What the model can't show:**

- **Behaviour outside the data sheet.** This covers supply below ±6 V, +IN below (V-)+1 V (which happens
  whenever -80 V is missing), and +IN below V- (the end state after losing -80 V). TI's model treats an input
  outside the CM range as if it sat at the CM limit. That choice produces the +14 V power-up excursion in
  item 4. The real part promises no phase reversal but doesn't specify these states, so take those numbers
  as plausible, not guaranteed.
- **Off rails.** A real +15 V regulator that is off and high impedance might be dragged negative by the op
  amps' supply current when -80 V is present. The model can't predict this: Iq near zero supply is the
  wrapper's assumption.
- **Thermal effects:** no thermal shutdown and no self-heating. Iq is fixed at 3.2 mA typ (Iq max is handled
  by hand in item 6).
- **Things not simulated:** noise, temperature drift (estimated by hand in item 1), and DAC7578 errors
  (sensitivity given).
- **A solver artefact.** The model has a second, non-physical DC solution: the output sits beyond V- and
  internal integrator nodes are at about 1e8 V. The operating-point solver sometimes lands on it. The runner
  checks every operating point (loop closed: |V(+IN) - V(-IN)| < 20 mV, and M80 at its source) and retries
  until it is right: seeded hints, then other solver options. Every result here comes from a checked
  operating point with the grid diode in place. `summary.json` → `solver_notes` lists the runs that needed a
  retry.

## 1 DC transfer

Nominal (model Vos 0.14 mV included; VREF2V5 2.4996 V under load):

| DAC | Ideal | BIASn | +IN | +IN to CM limits (V- +1 / V+ -3) | OUT to swing limits (V- +3 / V+ -1.5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 V | -70.000 V | -69.983 V | 0.000 V | 79.0 V / 12.0 V | 7.0 V / 83.5 V |
| 1.25 V | -36.650 V | -36.636 V | 1.150 V | 80.1 V / 10.8 V | 40.4 V / 50.1 V |
| 2.4994 V (FS) | -3.316 V | -3.306 V | 2.299 V | 81.3 V / 9.7 V | 73.7 V / 16.8 V |

Worst-case corners, all combinations, simulated. The corners are Rf and Rg ±0.1 %, VREF ±0.02 % (ADR4525B),
Vos ±3.4 mV (datasheet max, on top of the model's 0.14 mV), V- -78 / -82 V, V+ 14.25 / 15.75 V, and for DAC
codes above 0 the 10k / 115k ±1 %. That makes 64 corners at DAC 0 and 256 at each of the other points.

| DAC | Min (coldest) | Max (hottest) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 V | -70.236 V | **-69.731 V** |
| 1.25 V | -36.879 V | -36.395 V |
| FS | -3.538 V | -3.077 V |

- **Cold point.** Vos dominates: 3.4 mV × 29 = ±99 mV. The Rf / Rg ratio adds ±0.14 V and VREF ±14 mV. The
  supply corners move the output by less than 1 mV (PSRR).
- **Drift (by hand, not simulated).** Over a 50 K rise, the worst case is about ±0.2 V at the cold point: Vos
  20 µV/K × 29, 0.1 % resistors at 25 ppm/K each, and the ADR4525 at 2 ppm/K. That still leaves the cold
  point below -69.5 V.
- **DAC7578 errors are not included.** Each mV of DAC output error moves BIASn by 26.7 mV. The zero-code error
  can only be positive, so it can only make the cold point hotter.
- **Headroom.** At the -78 V corner and the coldest code, OUT is 7.8 V above V-. The datasheet's (V-)+3 V
  applies at 10 kΩ, and the load here is at least 280 kΩ DC plus at most 0.6 mA AC. Input CM: +IN stays
  0-2.3 V against -77 V to +11.25 V at the worst rails.
- **Grid leakage, ±5 µA into or out of the grid.** BIASn moves 5 mV (the current flows through the 1k, and
  OUT is held). The grid moves ±0.46 V (5 µA × 91k).

**Verdict: pass.**

## 2 Stability and step response

Loop gain by Tian's method: a voltage and a current injection at the op amp output pin, which breaks the
only loop (OUT → Rf ‖ Cf → -IN). The formula was checked against the exact return ratio of a test circuit.

| Case | Crossover | Phase margin | Gain margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **As drawn** (DAC 0, 1.25 V, FS: identical) | 5.34 MHz | **48.3°** | 16.3 dB |
| Without the grid network | 5.34 MHz | 48.3° | 16.3 dB |
| C2n2 derated to 0.25 µF (X7R at 70 V) | 5.34 MHz | 48.3° | 16.3 dB |
| Cf removed | 217 kHz | 81.2° | 30.7 dB |
| Cf 100 pF | 5.02 MHz | 52.0° | 17.7 dB |
| Cf 10 nF | 5.37 MHz | 47.9° | 16.2 dB |
| Cf 1 nF + 4.7k in series | 3.42 MHz | 30.3° | 6.8 dB |
| Cf 1 nF + 10k in series | 2.52 MHz | 33.9° | 8.5 dB |
| R2n4 100 Ω instead of 1k | 3.65 MHz | 62.3° | 23.2 dB |
| R2n4 0 Ω (1 µF directly on the output) | 121 kHz | **7.7°** | 20.7 dB |

What the table shows:

- **The 1k isolates the 1 µF and the grid network.** At crossover the load is the 1k, so the margin doesn't
  depend on the code, the tube side or the capacitor's derating. The 1k must stay: at 0 Ω the stage
  oscillates (7.7°).
- **The 48° is the OPA462's own unity-gain margin.** Above 568 Hz, Cf shorts Rf and the feedback factor goes
  to 1. The datasheet says the part is unity-gain stable.
- **Cf costs margin and buys nothing here.** Without it, the noise gain stays 29 and the margin is 81°. The
  filtering is already done by the 10k / 330n input (52 Hz) and the 1k / 1 µF output (159 Hz).
- **A resistor in series with Cf is worse.** It raises the -IN node impedance and makes a pole with the input
  capacitance near crossover. Don't do it.

Steps (`steps.png`):

| Step | BIASn overshoot | Settling (1 %) | Settling (1 LSB = 16 mV) | Op amp current peak | OUT slope max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 LSB at 1.25 V (163 mV) | 0 % | 17.4 ms | 8.6 ms | 0.14 mA | <0.001 V/µs |
| DAC 0 → FS (-70 → -3.3 V) | 0 % | 17.5 ms | 105 ms | 12.4 mA | 0.017 V/µs |
| DAC FS → 0 | 0 % | 17.5 ms | 105 ms | 12.7 mA | 0.017 V/µs |
| Loop only: 10 mV at +IN, 330n bypassed (diagnostic) | 16 % at OUT, gone in 1.2 µs | | | | |
| Loop only, Cf removed | none | | | | |

- **The response at BIASn is set by the filters, not by the loop.** These are the 52 Hz input filter and the
  159 Hz output filter. The loop's 16 % ring lasts about 1 µs and never reaches BIASn.
- **The 1-LSB tail of the large step (105 ms) is the coupling capacitor.** The 220 nF recharges through
  91k + 40k (τ = 29 ms), and that current (0.5 mA at first) drops across the 1k, outside the loop.
- **No slewing and no current limit.** The worst case is 12.7 mA against 45 mA, and 0.017 V/µs against
  32 V/µs.
- **A 1 mA grid-current step** moves BIASn 0.87 V colder: the current flows through the 1k, outside the loop.

**Verdict: pass.** Optional change P1 (remove Cf) raises the margin to 81°.

## 3 Drive coupling and blocking

PI plate at 280 V DC plus 70 V peak, through 40 kΩ, the 220 nF and the 91k, onto BIASn. DAC at 1.25 V
(BIASn -36.6 V). Small-signal AC values and transient checks:

| f | BIASn (peak) | OUT (peak) | Op amp current (peak) | BIASn with C2n2 at 0.25 µF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 Hz | 507 mV | 21 µV | 0.51 mA | 511 mV |
| 50 Hz | 503 mV | 21 µV | 0.50 mA | |
| 100 Hz | 449 mV | 22 µV | 0.45 mA | 523 mV |
| 1 kHz | 84 mV | 13 µV | 0.08 mA | 286 mV |
| 10 kHz | 8.5 mV | 1.7 µV | 0.008 mA | 34 mV |
| 20 kHz | 4.2 mV | 1.2 µV | 0.004 mA | 17 mV |

- **Transient with the full drive (20 Hz to 20 kHz).** The largest op amp current is 0.64 mA, at 20 Hz while
  the network settles. OUT moves at most 45 µV peak-to-peak, so there is no slewing and no current limit.
- **With the grid diode.** The undivided grid would reach +12 V: the 40k and 91k divide 70 V to 49 V on top of
  -36.6 V. The diode clamps it at +0.75 to +0.82 V. BIASn shifts by -25 to -34 mV mean (colder), and the
  ripple is about 7 % lower.
- **What the ripple means.** BIASn ripple is about 1 % of the drive at the grid. The bias node isn't an AC
  ground below about 160 Hz, which doesn't matter because the grid carries the full drive anyway. With
  C2n2 derated (likely for a 1 µF / 100 V X7R 0805 at 70 V), the mid-band ripple rises 3-4x. That is
  harmless, and the margin is unchanged.

Blocking-type grid current. The current source draws from the grid pin to the cathode, 1-5 mA for
1-10 ms, at DAC 1.25 V:

| Pulse | BIASn change | OUT change | Op amp current peak | Grid node (g1) shift | g1 back within 1 V |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 mA, 1 ms | 0.20 V | 14 µV | 0.14 mA | -30 V | 23 ms |
| 1 mA, 10 ms | 0.49 V | 21 µV | 0.35 mA | -47 V | 85 ms |
| 2 mA, 1 ms | 0.40 V | 28 µV | 0.27 mA | -60 V | 43 ms |
| 2 mA, 10 ms | 0.98 V | 41 µV | 0.84 mA | -93 V | 105 ms |
| 5 mA, 1 ms | 1.01 V | 71 µV | 0.88 mA | -150 V | 70 ms |
| 5 mA, 10 ms | 2.44 V | 103 µV | 2.31 mA | -233 V | 120 ms |

- **The op amp holds OUT to about 0.1 mV.** BIASn moves only by the current through the 1k, always colder.
- **The grid excursion and its recovery belong to the 220 nF / 91k / 40k network, not to the op amp.** An
  ideal current source overstates the shift: a real grid stops conducting as soon as it goes negative.

**Verdict: pass.**

## 4 Power-up sequencing

The DAC is held at 0 V throughout. VREF2V5 comes with +5 V, and E/D = +5 V (output disabled below about
0.6 V). The PI plate is at 0 V (no HV). Rails ramp in 10 ms and are staggered by 100 ms unless noted. Off
rails are held at 0 V by their source ("stiff"). The "1-quadrant" rows have regulators that only source
(+15 V) or only sink (-80 V), with 10 kΩ bleeders. `powerup.png` shows the main cases.

| Order (first → last) | Most positive BIASn | When | Cold (-69.5 V) after the last rail is up |
|---|---|---|---|
| +15, -80, +5 | **+4.2 V** | while only +15 V is up (output disabled, through 160 kΩ), then about 0 V until +5 V | 1.9 ms |
| +15, +5, -80 | **+14.4 V** | during the +5 V ramp: E/D has enabled, VREF still 0 | 10.5 ms |
| +5, +15, -80 | +0.17 V | while +15 V and +5 V are up without -80 V | 10.5 ms |
| -80, +15, +5 | 0 V (starts there, never above) | about 0 V while +5 V is absent | 1.2 ms |
| -80, +5, +15 | 0 V | -79.8 V (cold) until +15 V | before the +15 V ramp ends |
| +5, -80, +15 | 0 V | -79.8 V (cold) until +15 V | before the +15 V ramp ends |
| All together, 10 ms | +0.001 V | | 7.5 ms |
| All together, 200 ms | +0.002 V | | before the ramp ends |
| Slow (200 ms ramps, 100 ms stagger): +15, -80, +5 | +1.4 V | | before the ramps end |
| Slow: +15, +5, -80 | **+12.5 V** | | before the ramps end |
| Slow: +5, +15, -80 / -80, +15, +5 | +0.17 V / 0 V | | before the ramps end |
| 1-quadrant rails: +15, -80, +5 / -80, +15, +5 / +5, -80, +15 / together | +4.2 / 0 / +0.016 / +0.001 V | | as with stiff rails |

How the power-up behaves:

- **BIASn always starts at 0 V,** because the 1 µF is discharged. It stays near 0 V until both -80 V and VREF
  are present. It is cold within about 11 ms of the last rail being up.
- **Positive BIASn needs +15 V present and -80 V absent.** Then V- sits at +0.14 to +0.19 V: the S1G clamps
  the rail, because the op amps' supply current has nowhere else to go. +IN at 0 V is below the CM range,
  (V-)+1 = +1.1 V, and the model treats it as +1.1 V. So the output heads for 29 × 1.1 - 28 × VREF:
  - with VREF at 0, that is the positive rail (+14.9 V at OUT);
  - with VREF at 2.5 V, it is low (+0.17 V, just above V-).

  The +14 V cases come from E/D enabling at about 0.6 V on the +5 V ramp, while the ADR4525 needs about 3 V.
  See [Model](#model) on how far to trust the out-of-range behaviour.
- **Harmless as specified.** There is no HV, the heaters are cold, and the grid diode clamps the grid at
  about +0.7 V. The current is at most (14.4 - 0.7) V / 97 kΩ = 0.15 mA. The +15 V rail was not dragged
  negative in any case (minimum -3 mV), but see the model limits.
- **Driver B+ arriving later** (PI plate 0 → 280 V in 1 or 10 ms, op amp running, BIASn at -70 V): BIASn
  rises 0.9 V for a few tens of ms, and the op amp sources 1.2 mA.

**Verdict: attention, information only.** BIASn reaches +14 V if +15 V and +5 V come up before -80 V, and it
sits at about 0 V until -80 V and +5 V are both present. Without HV this is harmless. F4 (HV only after
-80 V and +15 V are good) already covers it. If even a cold-tube positive grid is unwanted, see P2.

## 5 Loss of -80 V

+15 V and +5 V stay present. At t = 0 the J201 source either opens (C201 then carries the whole 15 mA) or is
pulled to 0 V (C201 discharges through the 10R, τ ≈ 1 ms).

| Case | DAC | BIASn starts to move | **BIASn above -20 V** | Above -10 V | Above 0 V | End state |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source opens | 0 (-70 V) | 69 ms | **403 ms** | 471 ms | 542 ms | +9.5 V (max +10.7 V) |
| Source opens | 1.25 V (-36.6 V) | 293 ms | **403 ms** | 470 ms | 541 ms | +0.7 V |
| Source to 0 V | 0 | 0.7 ms | **3.4 ms** | 4.2 ms | 39 ms | +0.17 V |
| Source to 0 V | 1.25 V | 1.6 ms | **3.0 ms** | 4.0 ms | 22 ms | +0.16 V |

- **With the open source, the rail falls at 150 V/s.** It reaches -70 V after 65 ms, which matches the sheet
  note of 10 V in 60 ms. BIASn follows once V- comes within about 3 V of it.
- **The end state is positive.** M80 ends at +0.69 V, held by D201 against the op amps' supply current. The
  +0.7 to +10 V at BIASn is out-of-range model behaviour (+IN is then 0.7 V below V-), and the grid diode
  clamps the grid itself near +0.7 V. So the tube sees a slightly positive grid, not 0 V, until the FPGA
  removes HV.
- **Loss of +5 V, for comparison** (VREF2V5 and E/D go with it; DAC at 1.25 V, other rails present). BIASn
  passes -20 V after 39 ms and settles at 0 V if the DAC output collapses too. With the DAC held it ends at
  +5 V. The FPGA sees this as all ADCs gone (F3).
- **Loss of +15 V:** BIASn goes colder, to -79.8 V (+IN is then above the CM range). Safe.

**Information.**

## Proposals

**P1 (optional): fit C2n1 (Cf, 1 nF across Rf) as DNP.** It raises the phase margin from 48° to 81° and the
gain margin from 16 dB to 31 dB. The overshoot of the loop itself goes from 16 % to none. BIASn behaviour is
unchanged, because the 330n input and the 1k / 1 µF output already do the filtering. It also removes four
parts (D7).

- The drawn circuit is acceptable: 48° is the OPA462's specified unity-gain condition, and the ring never
  reaches BIASn.
- If Cf is kept, don't add a resistor in series with it (30-34°).
- Keep R2n4 at 100 Ω or more (0 Ω gives 7.7°).

**P2 (optional, only if a positive grid during power-up is unwanted even without HV).** Either of these
removes the +4 to +14 V excursions of item 4:

- Sequence the rails so that -80 V comes no later than +15 V. This goes into the PSU / control board
  requirements, next to F4.
- Or fit one diode per channel from BIASn to GND (anode at BIASn, a ≥ 100 V small-signal part), which clamps
  BIASn at about +0.6 V. In normal operation it sits reverse-biased at 3-70 V, with nA leakage. In the +14 V
  case the op amp would push about 14 mA into it through the 1k, which is inside its 45 mA limit.

  Not recommended unless wanted: the user's position is that the excursion is harmless without HV.

**Note, no change: C2n2 (1 µF / 100 V X7R 0805)** keeps only about 0.25 µF at 70 V. Stability is unaffected,
and the drive ripple at BIASn rises to 0.29 V peak at 1 kHz. A 1206 or a higher-voltage part keeps more
capacitance if wanted.
